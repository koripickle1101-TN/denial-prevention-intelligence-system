Core operating rule

Clearinghouse accepted ≠ payer accepted.

A successful clearinghouse transmission proves that one checkpoint was passed. It does not, by itself, prove payer receipt, payer acknowledgement, entry into adjudication, or final resolution.

Claim-Control Decision Logic

The control does not treat a transmitted claim as completed work. It evaluates whether downstream visibility and ownership exist.

Clearinghouse rejected

Hold at transmission checkpoint

Review the clearinghouse response and route the correction before treating the claim as downstream.

Clearinghouse accepted / payer unknown

Visibility incomplete

Confirm payer acknowledgement, claim presence, and next-action ownership.

Payer rejection / claim not found

Route an exception

Identify the supported exception category, assign ownership, document evidence, and set follow-up.

Payer acknowledged / adjudication active

Actively moving — not closed

Maintain status visibility and follow-up until final disposition is documented.

Final status received

Verify downstream closure

Final status alone is not enough if a denial, rejection, correction, or other action still needs ownership.

Closure verified

Controlled final state

The modeled final status, required downstream action, evidence trail, and closure are documented.

Control rule

A successful clearinghouse transmission is not completed claim progression.

DPIS treats the claim as controlled only when downstream payer acknowledgement and status visibility are established, or when an exception has a visible owner, next action, follow-up timing, evidence trail, and eventual closure.

Patient-to-Professional Perspective

“Why hasn't insurance processed my claim?”
“Why are you saying they never received it?”
“Why am I getting another bill?”
“Why can't anyone tell me where the claim is?”

The patient sees the waiting. Healthcare operations has to know where the claim actually is, what evidence proves that status, and who owns the next action.

How this extends DPIS

Pre-submission readiness → downstream claim control

Pre-Submission Claim Readiness Control™ asks whether the modeled claim is ready to leave the upstream workflow. Claim Transmission & Acknowledgement Control™ then asks whether the claim successfully moves through transmission, payer acknowledgement, adjudication visibility, exception handling, and closure.

When a downstream rejection or denial appears, the case can then move into Denial Traceback Review™ to investigate where the workflow may have first lost control.

Educational boundary

What this control does not do

This simulation does not transmit real claims, access a clearinghouse or payer, reproduce EDI transactions, determine payer acceptance, adjudicate claims, calculate reimbursement, interpret payer contracts, or make coding, coverage, medical-necessity, compliance, or legal determinations.
