Core operating principle

Automation can surface the account. Human review still controls the decision.

The simulated priority is never treated as decision authority. DPIS makes the signal, priority reason, evidence gaps, ownership, escalation, next action, override rationale, and closure status visible before a synthetic account is treated as controlled.

    Responsible automation principle

    Good automation should explain the work, not hide the decision.

    DPIS treats automation as a support layer. The modeled worker can see the triggering signals, priority rationale, recommended action, evidence gaps, ownership, escalation path, and override reason before the account moves.

    AI or automation can surface a signal, but the workflow still needs defined authority, ownership, escalation, documentation, and verification before that signal becomes accountable action.

    Evidence boundary

    No unsupported performance claims

    This portfolio module intentionally does not reuse unverified percentage claims about A/R reduction, collections, cost, or productivity. The simulation demonstrates workflow logic, not employer results or validated automation performance.

    Educational boundary

    What this module does not do

    It does not access real A/R, claims, payer portals, EHRs, contracts, bank data, or patient information. It does not determine coding, coverage, medical necessity, payer liability, legal rights, or the correctness of a real-world automated recommendation. All priorities and balances are fictional educational assumptions.
