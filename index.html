Core Operating Rule

A visible denial signal does not prove where the workflow first lost control. DPIS requires backward review, evidence-based reasoning, ownership, and closure verification before the case is treated as resolved.

02A

Upstream Requirement Analysis

DPIS extends root-cause analysis beyond the point where a denial becomes visible. When relevant, the model traces the failure upstream from the denial reason to the workflow step and then to the payer, documentation, authorization, coverage, contractual, or organizational requirement associated with that step.

Traceback Logic

Denial ← Claim failure ← Workflow failure ← Control failure ← Underlying requirement

Evaluate: What happened to denial performance?

Analyze: Why did the result occur and where did the failure originate?

Adjust: Modify the upstream control, workflow, escalation, or monitoring rule based on the identified cause.

Evidence boundary: Not every denial has a government-policy root cause. DPIS treats payer rules, contract terms, documentation requirements, authorization requirements, coverage conditions, and internal workflow design as distinct possible sources and names one only when the available evidence supports it.

03

Patient-to-Professional Perspective

Patients do not experience “authorization-service alignment,” “documentation readiness,” or “claim routing.” They experience another phone call, a delay, uncertainty about coverage, a confusing bill, or being asked to help untangle an administrative problem they did not create.

DPIS helps me practice connecting that patient-facing result to the earlier operational checkpoints that may need review.

The patient sees the waiting. Healthcare operations has to know where the claim actually is, what evidence proves that status, and who owns the next action.

The patient experiences the denial as an outcome. Healthcare operations has to determine whether resolving that account also reveals an earlier process gap that needs to be controlled.

The patient experiences the waiting. Operations has to make sure the account is visible to the right person before waiting becomes another unresolved patient problem.

The patient sees the delay. Healthcare operations has to determine where the claim actually stopped moving.

The patient sees the billing problem. Healthcare operations has to determine whether the coverage and payer-routing workflow was correct before the claim ever moved downstream.

The patient sees the balance. Healthcare operations has to understand the chain of transactions that produced it.

About This Portfolio Project

DPIS is a student-developed educational simulation built by Kori Pickle while working toward a Bachelor's of Science degree in Healthcare Administration. It uses fictional cases and synthetic values only.

It does not access an EHR, submit claims, determine coding accuracy, determine medical necessity, make coverage or payer decisions, calculate reimbursement, or represent employer deployment.

Operational Distinctions

Corrected claim ≠ corrected workflow.

Denial reason ≠ proven root cause.

Detection ≠ resolution.

Claim populated ≠ claim ready.

Correct data element ≠ complete workflow readiness.

Coverage verified ≠ payer order verified.

Correct coverage ≠ correct claim destination.

Payer destination selected ≠ payer responsibility established.

Clearinghouse accepted ≠ payer accepted.

Payer acknowledged ≠ adjudicated.

Adjudication started ≠ final disposition.

Status visible ≠ action completed.

Transmission successful ≠ claim actively progressing.

Claim sent ≠ claim received into adjudication.

No immediate rejection ≠ final acceptance.

Appeal submitted ≠ denial resolved.

Action taken ≠ closure verified.

Account resolved ≠ recurrence prevented.

Payment recovered ≠ workflow corrected.

Case closed ≠ process gap closed.

Immediate correction ≠ preventive improvement.

Process change ≠ proven improvement.

Automated priority ≠ verified next action.

Automated follow-up ≠ resolved account.

Queue position ≠ decision authority.

Oldest account ≠ automatically highest operational priority.

Paid ≠ correctly reconciled.

Reduced payment ≠ explanation of the underlying recovery.

Recovery posted ≠ recovery reconciled.

Adjusted balance ≠ reconciled account.

Recurrence is a signal for deeper review, not proof of a systemic cause.
